The ecosystem · packages/stripe

Charge from the first day

Stripe's hosted Checkout takes payments and starts subscriptions, its Customer Portal lets people change their plan or card or cancel, and a webhook keeps your database in step. Your app asks its own tables who has paid. Cards are typed on Stripe's pages, never yours.

The package handles the parts where small mistakes cost money: webhook signatures, events that come twice or late, one customer per person, and a thank-you page that knows before the event arrives. Your app keeps its prices (made in Stripe's Dashboard), its pages, and on_event!: what it does when someone pays.

A first app#

Each file below is from packages/stripe/example, which sells a subscription (Basic or Pro) and an e-book, with the auth package's dev sign-in. Its tests pay every way, on SQLite and Postgres, against a stand-in for Stripe's API.

1. The plugin and the webhook. List the plugin, for its tables. The server needs STRIPE_SECRET_KEY and STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET to start; under --dev it starts without them, with Stripe off, as it does without the AI's key. The webhook is your route (D-116), calling the package's handler with your on_event!.

2. Off to Checkout. A plan's button posts its price to your endpoint, which calls Stripe.checkout! and redirects. The mode comes from your plan, never the form. Each user gets one Stripe customer, made the first time. A price may be named by its lookup key, set in the Dashboard: Stripe.price!("pro_monthly") is its id, the same key in test and live mode.

3. Back from Checkout. Stripe redirects as soon as it is paid, often before its events. Stripe.checkout_session! fetches the session (by the ?session_id= the package put in the success URL) and keeps it, so this page already knows.

4. When someone pays. on_event! gets each event after the package keeps it, in the same transaction. Here a bought e-book becomes an order; a failed renewal is logged:

5. Pages for those who paid. Middleware checks Stripe.subscribed! for the subscribers' pages and Stripe.paid_for! for the e-book; anyone else goes to the pricing page.

Each guards a group (a directory in brackets, which adds nothing to the paths): the Pro page and its endpoint are in routes/(subscribers)/, behind its +middleware.roc:

6. The portal. A button on the account page opens Stripe's Customer Portal, which comes back when they are done. Their changes arrive as events.

Developing with the Stripe CLI#

Use test mode: sk_test_ keys, nothing charged, and the test card 4242 4242 4242 4242 (any future date, any CVC). In the Dashboard, in test mode, make your products and prices (checkout! takes their price_… ids), and save the Customer Portal's settings once: until then, Stripe makes no portal sessions in test mode. Forward events with the Stripe CLI:

In production, add a webhook endpoint in the Dashboard (Developers, Webhooks) at <public URL>/stripe/webhook, and give the server its signing secret. Send it the checkout.session.*, customer.subscription.*, invoice.paid, invoice.payment_failed, customer.deleted, charge.refunded and charge.dispute.* events. A one-time payment refunded in full or disputed no longer counts as paid (a dispute won gives it back). At start the server logs its keys' mode, and warns about live keys under --dev.

How a payment goes#

Stripe.checkout!({ user, email, mode, prices, success_url, cancel_url }) finds or makes the user's Stripe customer (your id in its metadata) and a Checkout Session with the user as its client_reference_id. After the success page (step 3), events reach the webhook: checkout.session.completed, customer.subscription.created, invoice.paid. Renewals, failed payments, plan changes and portal cancellations arrive the same way later, and Stripe.subscribed! follows them.

Option of checkout!Is
user : Stryour id for whoever pays
email : Strtheir address, for a customer made now (optional)
mode : [Payment, Subscription]once, or every period
prices : List({ price : Str, quantity : U64 })Stripe's price ids, and how many
success_url, cancel_urla path of your site, or a URL; the success URL gets session_id={CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}
trial_days : U64a subscription's free days first (optional)
promotion_codes : Boolwhether a promotion code may be typed (optional)
metadata : List((Str, Str))more of your own, on the session (optional)

The webhook#

Stripe.webhook_route!(request, on_event!) answers POST /stripe/webhook from your route file, behind its folder's middleware, which must let Stripe's servers in (webhook_at tells the plugin the path, for its log line at start). For each request:

  • The signature. Stripe-Signature must hold the HMAC-SHA256, under your webhook secret, of its timestamp and the exact body, at most five minutes old; anything else is a 400. While you roll a secret, Stripe signs with each; one match is enough.
  • Once. The event's id is recorded in the transaction that handles it. A repeat (Stripe resends until answered) is answered, not handled again; two copies at once wait for each other.
  • Kept, then yours. The package keeps what the event says (a checkout's state, a subscription's, a deleted customer), then gives on_event! the event, read: its type, its user, and Session, Subscription, Invoice or Other. Its Err rolls everything back and answers 500, so Stripe tries again later, for up to three days.
  • In any order. Stripe promises none. A subscription's row keeps the version, time and kind of the event that last changed it, so a late event never brings back an older state, and an ended subscription stays ended. For two changes in one second, the package asks Stripe which is last.

Keep slow work out of on_event!: queue a job for the receipt or the email, and it commits with the event.

Who has paid#

These read your database, never Stripe: a page that checks them waits on no other server, and nothing the browser sends decides who has paid.

FunctionSays
Stripe.subscribed!(user)whether they have a subscription that is active, trialing, or past due while Stripe tries the card again
Stripe.paid_for!(user, what)whether such a subscription has a price or product (price_…, prod_…), or a paid one-time payment was for the price and hasn't been refunded in full or disputed since
Stripe.subscription!(user)their subscription: status, items, when it renews or ends, whether it ends then
Stripe.payments!(user)their one-time payments that were paid, and not refunded in full or disputed since
Stripe.portal!(user, return_url)the URL of their Customer Portal, or NoCustomer

For the rest of Stripe's API (a refund, a list of invoices), Stripe.post! and Stripe.get! call it as the package does: the secret key, a pinned Stripe-Version, an idempotency key on a create, a retry after a network error. That key is new each call. When the app may ask twice (a job run again, a form sent twice), Stripe.post_with! takes your own key, so Stripe creates once.

Testing without Stripe#

STRIPE_API_URL points the package at another server. The example's tests start a stand-in for Stripe's API (example/test/stand-in.mjs, Node with no dependencies): the endpoints the package calls, answering with Stripe's objects, and what paying on Checkout's page would do. Each test signs an event as Stripe does and sends it to the webhook. Copy it into your app's tests.

The tables#

The plugin's migrations (stripe/0001_stripe, then stripe/0002_refunds) make four tables, the same on SQLite and Postgres: stripe_customers (each user's Stripe customer), stripe_checkouts, stripe_subscriptions (as the events last said) and stripe_events (those handled, swept after 30 days). Your users stay in your table; the package knows them by your id.

Safety#

  • No card reaches your server: Checkout and the portal are Stripe's pages.
  • The webhook takes signed events only, compared in constant time, at most five minutes old.
  • A checkout belongs to its user: the success page refuses another user's session.
  • Every create carries an idempotency key, so a retried request makes one customer, one session.
  • Test and live keys are told apart at start, and live keys under --dev are a warning.

A routes/+middleware.roc that requires sign-in everywhere would refuse Stripe's webhook too: guard your routes by directory instead. The webhook has no Origin, so the platform's cross-site check lets it through.
