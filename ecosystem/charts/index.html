The ecosystem · packages/charts

Charts that glide to their data, and then stop

Seven SVG charts for a dashboard, a report or a live tally: bar, stacked bar, line, sparkline, donut, scatter and heatmap. The server renders them; the browser hydrates them like any component. When data changes they glide to it, a frame at a time, then nothing runs. Every chart here is live: Randomize gives it new data, Auto (on from the start) does so every 3.3 seconds, and Neon, Blueprint and Paper restyle it with CSS custom properties alone.

Installing it#

Name the package in your app's header (main.roc), by its directory's name:

rocx build then copies each chart into your app; in a .rocx file, import charts.BarChart imports it (the ecosystem's overview explains why). A chart needs its data and a few words about it:

Every chart takes the same three kinds of prop:

  • data!, an accessor prop: the chart calls it to read the data and follows its changes. Write any expression that reads your signals, like data!={rain!()}. Values are Dec, Roc's decimal.
  • label, what the chart shows, for screen readers (label! on <Sparkline>, an accessor, since it changes with the data).
  • format, which writes a value as text for ticks and readouts: Format.short (1.3k) unless you give your own. <LineChart> also has format_x; <ScatterChart> has format_x and format_y instead; <Sparkline> writes no values.

In use: Plate (each day's energy as bars, weight as a line), the quiz (how the room answered, as bars) and Uptime (response times as a line, failures as a heatmap, a sparkline on each monitor's card).

<BarChart>#

Roc is idle: the bars are where the data says.

PropIs
data! : () => List({ label : Str, value : Dec }) A bar per item, in order. A bar belongs to its label: a new value glides it to its new height; a new label grows a bar from the axis while the others make room; a gone label's bar shrinks into the axis and fades (its <rect>'s transition:out) as the others close up. A new order slides each bar to its place.
label : StrWhat the chart shows, for screen readers.
format : (Dec -> Str) ?? Format.shortA value as text: on the ticks, and on a bar when it is pointed at or focused.
padding : Dec ?? 0.3The share of each bar's slot left empty. Blueprint's thin bars use 0.55, Paper's wide ones 0.12.

The axis runs from zero (or the least value, if negative) to the greatest, widened to round numbers. Tab reaches each bar; a screen reader reads its label and value. Two items with the same label are two bars.

<StackedBarChart>#

Roc is idle: the columns are where the data says.

PropIs
data! : () => List({ label : Str, values : List(Dec) }) A column per item, one value per series stacked from the axis up. A column belongs to its label: its parts glide to their new sizes, a new column grows from the axis while the others make room, and a gone label's column shrinks into the axis and fades. A value below zero counts as zero.
series : List(Str)The series' names, bottom one first. The legend over the chart shows each in its colour.
label : StrWhat the chart shows, for screen readers.
format : (Dec -> Str) ?? Format.shortA value as text: on the ticks, and a column's total when it is pointed at or focused.
padding : Dec ?? 0.3The share of each column's slot left empty.

The axis runs from zero to the tallest column, widened to round numbers. Tab reaches each column; a screen reader reads its label and each series' value.

<LineChart>#

Roc is idle: the line is where the data says.

PropIs
data! : () => List({ x : Dec, y : Dec }) The points, left to right: across by x (a day, an hour, a time in milliseconds), to scale, and up by y. When they change, the line glides to them; if their number changes, it jumps instead. For evenly spaced values, LineChart.evenly(values) puts them one apart, as the demo's hours are.
label : StrWhat the chart shows, for screen readers.
format : (Dec -> Str) ?? Format.shortA value as text: on the ticks up the side, and over the point under the pointer.
format_x : (Dec -> Str) ?? Format.shortAn x as text: on the ticks along the bottom, and the point's under the pointer.
smooth : Bool ?? Bool.FalseMonotone curves through the points (they never overshoot one) instead of straight segments.

The side axis runs from the least value to the greatest, widened to round numbers. The bottom one runs from the least x to the greatest, ticked on round numbers, so points on irregular days sit as far apart as the days: Plate draws its weight so, each day a number (Date.days). Pointing along the chart shows the nearest point, its value over the plot and its x on the axis. The pointer finds points by where the data puts them, not where the line is drawn, so a gliding line doesn't move them.

<Sparkline>#

Visitors 898 ▼ 18%
Sign-ups 63 ▼ 16%
Orders 165 ▼ 41%

Roc is idle: the lines are where the data says.

PropIs
data! : () => List(Dec)The values, left to right. When they change, the line glides to them.
label! : () => StrWhat it shows, for screen readers: with no axis, say the trend. An accessor, since it changes with the data.

A small line with no axis, shaded underneath, with a dot on the last value, to sit beside a number. It spans its values' range, so it shows their shape, not their size. The tiles around these are the demo's, not the package's.

<DonutChart>#

Roc is idle: the slices are where the data says.

PropIs
data! : () => List({ label : Str, value : Dec }) A slice per item, clockwise from twelve o'clock, its share of the ring its share of the total. A slice belongs to its label: a new value glides it to its new share, a new label grows a slice from nothing while the others make room, and a gone label's slice shrinks to nothing where it was. A value below zero counts as zero.
label : StrWhat the chart shows, for screen readers.
format : (Dec -> Str) ?? Format.shortA value as text: the total, and each slice's value in the legend.
total : Str ?? "Total"The word under the total.
thickness : Dec ?? 52The ring's width, out of a radius of 150. Blueprint's 24 is a thin ring; Paper's 150 is a pie. Above 90 there is no room in the middle, so the total isn't shown.

The middle shows the total, or a slice's share while you point at it, focus it (Tab reaches each) or point at its legend line. A slice keeps its colour as others come and go; a new slice takes a colour no other has.

<ScatterChart>#

Roc is idle: the dots are where the data says.

PropIs
data! : () => List({ label : Str, x : Dec, y : Dec }) A dot per item, across by x and up by y. A dot belongs to its label, so new values glide it to its new place. A new dot swells from nothing; a gone label's dot shrinks away (a transition both ways).
label : StrWhat the chart shows, for screen readers.
format_x : (Dec -> Str) ?? Format.shortA value across: on the ticks along the bottom, and on a dot when it is pointed at or focused.
format_y : (Dec -> Str) ?? Format.shortA value up: on the ticks up the side, and on a dot.

Each axis runs from least to greatest, widened to round numbers. Tab reaches each dot; a screen reader reads its label and values.

<Heatmap>#

Roc is idle: the browser fades the cells.

PropIs
data! : () => List(List(Dec))A list of values for each row, one value per column. A cell's colour goes from --chart-heat-low at zero to --chart-heat-high at the greatest value.
rows : List(Str)The rows' labels, down the side.
columns : List(Str)The columns' labels. Each appears in the readout; along the bottom there is room for about eight, so with more columns only every second or third is shown.
label : StrWhat the chart shows, for screen readers.
format : (Dec -> Str) ?? Format.shortA value as text: the scale's ends, and the cell pointed at.

Under the grid are the colour scale and a readout of the cell under the pointer (row, column, value); that cell's outline is drawn on top, so no neighbour covers it. A new value changes a cell's strength (a custom property), and a CSS transition fades it to its new colour, so Roc runs no frames: the line under the demo stays at zero.

How they move#

A chart keeps what it has drawn in a signal, apart from its data. While the two differ, a hole's if holds an element with an on:frame.ms handler that, once per animation frame, moves the drawing a share of the remaining way (Ease.toward). Once the drawing reaches the data, the if turns false, the element goes, and the frames stop. The line under each demo counts them.

Marks are kept by label, so new data moves the mark you see instead of making another. A mark that comes or goes plays a transition, which the browser runs. The heatmap leaves even the glide to the browser.

The buttons are the demos', not the package's. Randomize sets the data's signal. Auto starts a set_interval! and keeps the clear! it returns in a signal, so pressing Auto again stops it.

The buttons and the looks, and the demos' data: site/Demos/Shuffle.rocx and the seven demos

Styling, three ways#

Each chart is width: 100% (a donut and its legend share it, wrapping when narrow). Colours, lines and font come from CSS custom properties, falling back to the text's colour or a plain default. So a look is a few lines of CSS on any element around the charts. Neon, Blueprint and Paper are three: each one rule on the demo's stage, plus a prop or two the demo changes with it (the bars' padding, the line's smooth, the donut's thickness).

PropertySets
--chart-color, --chart-activeThe colour of bars, lines and dots; the colour of a bar or dot pointed at, and of the line chart's point.
--chart-1 to --chart-5The colours of a donut's slices and a stacked chart's series. A colour stays with its slice or series.
--chart-heat-low, --chart-heat-highA heatmap's colours at zero and at the greatest value.
--chart-fill, --chart-stroke, --chart-stroke-width, --chart-outlineThe marks' fill opacity and outline. With --chart-outline: 1, a slice or a part is outlined in its own colour instead of the surface's.
--chart-radius, --chart-dot, --chart-line-width, --chart-areaBars' rounded corners, dots' radius, lines' width, and the shading under a line (0 for none).
--chart-grid, --chart-grid-dash, --chart-axis, --chart-mutedThe grid and its dashes, the axis, and the text of ticks and labels.
--chart-surface, --chart-font, --chart-glowThe background behind the marks (it shows in the gaps between slices and parts), the font, and a filter on the marks (Neon's drop-shadow).

The arithmetic#

The components draw with plain functions on no platform, which you can use for your own charts. Numbers are Dec, so the ticks from 0 to 1 are exactly 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8. roc test packages/charts/main.roc runs their expects; the README lists every function.

ModuleWhat it does
ScaleScale.linear(domain, range) maps values to places: scale.at(value) is a place, scale.invert(place) a value. .nice(count) widens the domain to round numbers, .ticks(count) lists them, and Scale.extent(values) is the least and the greatest. Scale.strips(places, range) gives each place the stretch nearer it than its neighbours (how the line chart's pointer finds points).
BandBand.band(count, range, padding) gives a slot to each bar: .at(slot), .width(), .center(slot). A count and a slot may be fractions, as they are while a chart glides from seven bars to six.
TicksAbout count ticks at 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten.
PathSVG path data: line, smooth (monotone curves), area, smooth_area, and arc (a donut's slice).
FormatNumbers as labels: num(x, places) ("2.5", not "2.50"), short ("1.3k"), percent, and coord (to hundredths, for SVG).
Easetoward(from, to, ms, close): one frame's step of a glide, which finishes once it is within close.
KeysWhat keeps a mark its own: unique (a key per label, Jan#1 for a second Jan), merge (the new keys, with the ones on their way out kept where they were), slots (a colour that stays with its key).

Limits, for now#

  • Values are Dec, and Roc has no F64 to Dec conversion yet. Round to an integer and divide: (x * 1000.0).round_to_i64_try(), then .to_dec() / 1000.0.
  • Donuts and stacked bars have five colours, so a sixth slice or series shares one: fold the smallest into "Other". When new data reorders a donut's slices, they jump rather than glide.
  • Tab can't reach a heatmap's cells or a line chart's or sparkline's points; to a screen reader, each is one image, named by its label.
  • A line chart's ticks along the bottom fall on round numbers of its x: days since 1970 in steps of 1, 2, 5, 10 or 20, not each Monday or the first of each month.
  • rocx requires HTML tags in lower case, so SVG's camel-cased elements (<linearGradient>, <clipPath>) can't be written yet.
