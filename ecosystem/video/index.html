The ecosystem · packages/video

Videos made of your components

A composition is a component that reads the frame it is at and renders that moment. The same composition plays live in a page and renders, a frame at a time, to an MP4 or a WebM. It is made of your real components, so when one of them changes, its videos change with it.

This is Remotion's idea, for Roc Solid. Use it for a landing page's animation that shows the product's real screens, for the same thing as a file to post, and for a short video of a release. A frame is worked out from its number, so it looks the same however it is reached: played, scrubbed to, or rendered.

Installing it#

Name the package in the app's header:

Then import video.Player, video.Sequence and video.Timeline in a .rocx file. rocx build copies the two components into the app (Generated/video/), as it does for the charts. The package isn't a plugin: it adds nothing to the server. Rendering a file needs Node, Chromium's headless shell and ffmpeg on the machine that renders.

Writing a scene#

A scene reads its clock with Player.clock!({}) in its body: the frame, as a getter, and the frames a second. It reads the frame where it is tracked, in a hole, a computed attribute or a memo, so only what depends on it changes each frame. Inside a sequence, the frame counts from the sequence's start, so a scene is written as if it starts at 0:

Three things are worth knowing:

  • Give a pure component a memo, not the frame. A pure component given a value made from the frame is made again when that value changes. Write ticked! = create_memo!(|| frame!() >= 14) and pass ticked!(): a memo compares its value with ==, so the component is made once per change, and its own CSS transitions play.
  • A computed style doesn't multiply. A style's value may hold letters, digits, spaces and .,%#()-+, so calc(… * …) is dropped. Pass the number as a custom property (style:--row={row!().to_str()}) and do the arithmetic in the stylesheet.
  • Components keep their own motion. CSS transitions, transition:in and transition:out, and a component's own on:frame animation (the chart's glide) all play: in a page in real time, and in a render on the renderer's clock.

The player#

<Player> holds the frame and plays it. The server renders its poster frame, and so does the browser as it hydrates. On its first animation frame it plays from the start, again and again, unless told otherwise.

PropWhat it is
framesHow long it is, in frames.
fpsFrames a second. 30 unless you set it.
width, heightThe video's size, in CSS pixels: what a render films, and the stage's size when it is scaled. 1280 by 720 unless you set them.
fitFlow (the default) lays the composition out as any markup, at the player's width. Scale lays it out at its width and height and scales it to fit, as a video is.
autoplay, loopPlays from the start once the page is live, and again when it ends. Both on unless you turn them off; without loop, it stops on its last frame.
posterThe frame shown before it plays, and where nothing plays it: Start, End (the default) or Frame(n).
controlsCorner (the default): a play and pause button in its corner. Bar: the button, a scrubber and the time. Hidden: nothing to see. Anything that moves by itself for more than five seconds needs a way to pause it, so keep one on a page.
labelIts aria-label.
its childrenThe composition.

Its element says where it is: data-frame, data-playing, and data-live once the page is live. Its scrubber, input.video-seek, is there even when hidden: set it to a frame and dispatch input, and the player goes there and pauses. The stories' catalog, the renderer and tests drive it that way.

Sequences#

<Sequence from={90} frames={60}> shows what is inside from frame 90 for 60 frames, and gives it a clock that counts from 0 there. Without frames, it lasts to the end. What is inside is made as its stretch starts and goes as it ends, so its transitions in and out play there. With hold, it shows all along instead: before its stretch as at its first frame, after it as at its last. Three steps side by side use that to wait their turn and then stay done. Sequences nest.

Timeline#

FunctionWhat it gives
progress(frame, from, to)How far along the span the frame is: 0 at from and before, 1 at to and after.
eased(frame, from, to, Out)The same, eased: Linear, In, Out, InOut or Back (past the goal and back).
tween(frame, from, to, a, b, easing)A value going from a to b over the span.
keys(frame, [(0, 0.0), (10, 1.0)])A value through keyframes, steadily from each to the next; keys_with eases each move.
spring(frame, fps)A spring let go at frame 0: past 1 a little, settled in about a second. spring_with takes its stiffness, damping and mass.
since(frame, 30)The frames since frame 30, and 0 before it: to start a spring there.
typed(text, share)The first share of a text's characters, typed a character at a time.
mix, round, frames_ofA share of the way between two values, a count as it climbs, and seconds in frames.

Rendering a file#

rocx render opens the page in Chromium's headless shell and lets it load and hydrate. Then it takes the page's clock over: performance.now(), Date, timers and animation frames move only when it says. For each frame, it moves the clock on a frame, seeks the player there, waits for the page to settle, and puts every running animation where the clock says. That includes the platform's transitions and CSS's. Then it photographs the player's stage, and ffmpeg makes the pictures a video.

So a frame doesn't depend on how long it took to film, and two renders of one composition are the same, frame for frame. ffmpeg writes H.264 into .mp4 and VP9 into .webm. A path ending in / gets one PNG for each frame. --scale 2 (the default) films at twice the CSS size. --player picks a player by index or label, and --from and --to film part of it. ffmpeg is $FFMPEG, the one on PATH, or Nix's.

In the stories#

A story with a player in it is a video; nothing else marks it. In the stories' catalog, a transport appears above it. It has play and pause (Space), a frame back and on (← and →; a second with Shift), a scrubber, the time and the frame, a speed and looping. Every player the catalog shows follows that one clock. A component's stories side by side therefore play in step, and so do stories you compare.

To compare stories, pin any of them in the side (or press P on one), from any component and of either kind. Then open "Compare … side by side". Render copies the command that films each video shown into an MP4 and a WebM.

Not yet#

  • A player plays whatever the reader's prefers-reduced-motion says, since Roc can't read a media query yet. Its button pauses it.
  • A player starts when the page goes live, not when it scrolls into view.
  • A demo is driven by its props from the frame, not by real clicks sent at given frames.
  • Rendering is a Node script, not a rocx command. It renders on your machine, not on the server with an app's data, and videos are silent.
